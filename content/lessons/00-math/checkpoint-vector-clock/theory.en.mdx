## Goal

This is Track 0's first mini-build: the trig formula you need is provided right in the prompt; the rest is a working artifact that proves you actually command coordinates, unit vectors and scaling — not just recognizing the formulas, but using them to build something visible and running.

## Requirements

Build a working analog clock on a 2D canvas that tells real system time, no external libraries. 12 hour marks arranged in a circle, placed via $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ (the hands need the canvas-adjusted axes — $x=\sin\theta$, $y=-\cos\theta$ — see the hint); hour/minute/second hands take their angles from `new Date()` and animate smoothly via `requestAnimationFrame`. Full details live in the Exercises section below — read the requirements there closely before starting.

## Structure Hint

Everything here is built around one function: `hand(angle, length)`, returning a hand's endpoint = clock center + a unit vector at `angle`, scaled by `length`. Hour marks, hour hand, minute hand, second hand — all four are just different calls to this same function with different angles and lengths.

## What "Done" Looks Like

Check your result against the checklist in the exercises section before opening the solution — 12 hour marks in the right places, the second hand completing a full turn in exactly 60 seconds, the hour hand drifting smoothly between numerals (no stepping), and everything running on `requestAnimationFrame`, not `setInterval`.
